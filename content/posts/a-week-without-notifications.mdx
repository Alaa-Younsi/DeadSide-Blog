The setup was deliberately unambitious: no app deletions, no phone left in a drawer, nothing dramatic — just every notification turned off for seven days, badges included, so the phone would only ever say something if it was opened on purpose. The hypothesis going in was that the week would feel calmer. That part turned out to be true, but it wasn't the interesting part.

The interesting part was day three, when the habit of checking hadn't gone anywhere — it had just lost its trigger. The hand still reached for the phone at the same moments it always had, on the same rough schedule, notification or not. The itch wasn't being caused by the badges. The badges were just one of several things that could scratch it, and removing one didn't remove the itch, it just left it looking for a different door.

By the end of the week the checking frequency had actually dropped, but not because of anything the settings menu did. It dropped because, without the badge supplying an answer in advance, opening the phone to find nothing waiting got boring fast enough that the hand stopped reaching. The notifications weren't the addiction. They were just the fastest, least effortful reward available — and removing the fast option quietly made the whole loop less worth running.
