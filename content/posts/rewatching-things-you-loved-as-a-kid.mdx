There's a particular risk in revisiting anything you loved before you had any taste to speak of. The thing itself hasn't changed — the film is the same reel, the show is the same episodes in the same order — but you have, and the gap between those two facts is where the discomfort lives. Sometimes what made it good is still there, just quieter than memory made it. Sometimes what made it good was never really the thing at all, but the specific, unrepeatable circumstances of watching it for the first time.

The honest move is to let both be true without needing a verdict. A story doesn't have to hold up structurally to have been formative. It just has to have been there at the right moment, doing the specific work that moment needed. Grading it against an adult's standards years later isn't really criticism — it's a category error, applying the wrong instrument to the wrong question.

What's worth paying attention to instead is the shape of what stuck. Not "was this good," but "what did this teach me to want from a story," because that answer usually says more about the person doing the rewatching than it does about whatever's playing on the screen.
