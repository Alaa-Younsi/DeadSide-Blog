The plan was simple and, in hindsight, far too confident: get from one city to another in a single day, by the most direct route available, with enough buffer to account for the usual small delays. The delay that actually showed up wasn't small, and it wasn't on the schedule at all — a closed road, a rerouted bus, and suddenly the direct path had a detour stitched into the middle of it that added most of a day.

What's strange is how little of that day I actually remember resenting. There was a town along the detour that wasn't on any itinerary, with a market that only made sense at that specific hour, full of people who had no idea they were, for about forty minutes, the entire content of someone's trip. None of it would have happened on the route that worked.

<PullQuote>
  Every trip I actually remember has a detour in it somewhere. The ones that went exactly to plan mostly didn't.
</PullQuote>

I don't think this generalizes into a tidy life lesson about embracing chaos — most delays are just delays, and most detours are just worse versions of the original route. But every so often the interruption is where the actual story was hiding the whole time, waiting for the schedule to fail first.
