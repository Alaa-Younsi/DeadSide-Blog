There's a specific kind of fear that comes with hitting publish on something unfinished — not unfinished in the sense of a broken link, but unfinished in the sense that the opinion in it might not survive contact with next month's version of you. For a long time, that fear was enough to keep most of my writing in private notes, half-drafts that existed only to be reread and never sent anywhere.

What changed wasn't confidence. It was the realization that the private drafts weren't actually safer — they were just unaccountable. A thought that never leaves a notes app never gets to be wrong in a way that teaches you anything. It just sits there, quietly certain of itself, never tested.

<PullQuote cite="a note to self, six months in">
  The goal was never to be right on the first draft. It was to leave a trail I could actually argue with later.
</PullQuote>

So the habit became: write the thing, let it be a little rough, publish it anyway, and let time do the editing that fear was trying to do in advance. Some of it holds up. Some of it reads now like a snapshot of a position I've since abandoned, which is its own kind of useful — a dated record of how the thinking moved, rather than a single frozen "correct" answer.

The unexpected part wasn't the writing getting better, though it did. It was how much easier it became to change my mind in public once there was a paper trail showing the change was earned rather than hidden. A newspaper runs corrections. It doesn't pretend the first printing never happened.
